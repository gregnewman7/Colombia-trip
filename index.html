🧭 See & do

🚶 Where to wander

Chapinero / Zona G, Parque 93 and Usaquén are the neighborhoods where a daytime stroll and an early dinner feel easiest for visitors. Ask reception at the apartment which blocks they’d walk.

🙅 Maybe skip

  • Walking up Monserrate (steep at altitude). The funicular and cable car are the fun way.
  • La Candelaria after dark, and the hillside streets above it.
  • Street-hailed taxis, rides offered by strangers, and “guides” who approach you.
  • Street emerald sellers and money changers.
  • Marches and demonstrations. If a crowd is forming, just change direction.

Need a hand? 123

123 reaches police, ambulance and fire anywhere in Colombia. Say “Emergencia”, then the address or the nearest landmark.

If plans go sideways

Quick reads. Tap to open.

Something feels off
  • Trust that feeling. Leave first, explain later. You never owe anyone politeness.
  • Step into the nearest busy, well-lit place (hotel lobby, pharmacy, café, mall) and ask staff to stay near you.
  • Order a ride from inside, and let someone at home know where you are.
Phone or bag taken
  • Hand it over. Not worth chasing or arguing.
  • Get somewhere comfortable, then use your friend’s phone to tell home you’re fine.
  • Later: lock or erase the phone remotely, freeze cards, and file a police report (you’ll need it for insurance).
You get separated
  • Stay put in a safe public place rather than wandering to look for each other.
  • Message on WhatsApp and share your live location.
  • Meet at the apartment or a landmark you can both name.
  • No contact after 20–30 minutes? Call a local contact.
A worrying call about one of you

If someone calls claiming one of you is hurt or in trouble, it’s almost always a scam that targets US families.

  • Hang up, and don’t send money.
  • Check with each other directly on WhatsApp or text.
  • Use your family code word. A real caller won’t know it.
Feeling unwell

Altitude: Bogotá is about 2,640 m (8,660 ft) and Monserrate is higher. A headache, tiredness or mild queasiness in the first day or two is common. Sit down, drink water, slow the pace and skip steep walks.

Head to a hospital (or call 123) for confusion, a severe headache, trouble breathing at rest, repeated vomiting, chest pain or fainting.

If someone suddenly feels woozy or foggy after accepting food or a drink from someone they just met, treat it as urgent: stay together, get to staff or a public place, and call 123.

A ride doesn’t feel right
  • Don’t get in if the plate, car or driver doesn’t match the app.
  • Already in? Ask to stop somewhere busy, step out, and share your trip in the app.
  • Skip rides offered on the street or at the airport.
Passport trouble
  • File a police report (denuncia) and keep a copy.
  • Call the US Embassy and say you need an emergency passport.
  • A photo of the passport page on your phone speeds things up.

🏥 Hospitals near the apartment

Large private clinics with 24-hour emergency rooms. Bring travel-insurance details and a credit card.

🧳 Our travel rules

  • Stay together at all times.
  • Avoid walking at night, and avoid isolated streets and areas.
  • Use planned transportation (Uber) rather than taxis.
  • Keep phones charged.
  • Keep passports and important documents secure.
  • Carry only the cash you need, and don’t flash jewelry, watches or electronics.
  • Keep bags and purses closed and in front of you in crowds.
  • Stay aware of your surroundings.
  • Don’t accept food or drinks from strangers.
  • No alcohol, especially in an unfamiliar country.
  • If a situation feels wrong, leave right away rather than worrying about being polite.

🌆 Bogotá street smarts

  • “No dar papaya.” The local saying for not making yourself an easy target. Phone-snatching (often by passing motorbikes) is the classic one: take your phone out briefly, away from the curb, ideally in a doorway.
  • Use ATMs only inside malls or banks.
  • La Candelaria is lovely by day. Head out before dark.
  • Be wary of anyone unusually friendly who offers to show you around, sells emeralds on the street or wants to exchange money.
  • If there’s a march or demonstration, steer clear and re-route.
  • Lock the apartment door, and call reception before opening it to someone unexpected.
  • Sealed bottles and busy sit-down places over street food. Bogotá tap water is generally considered safe, but bottled is fine.
